Rethinking Recycling

Recycling is good for consumers, manufacturers, and the planet overall. It protects the environment by using less finite resources from earth, and protects the economy from growing manufacturing costs. Recycling reduces energy consumption by up to 80%, reduces emissions by up to 70%, and are responsible for over 680,000 jobs in the US alone. If recycling is so great, how can we improve the amount that actually gets recycled?

Closing the gaps with Extended Producer Responsibility

EPR means that businesses are held financially and operationally responsible for the entire lifecycle of their products. This means that local governments and taxpayers no longer pay for waste management costs. The more that manufacturers are on the hook, the less toxic materials will be used. This needs to be passed at a state and federal level to be beneficial to the environment.

Recycling Literacy:
What goes in the bin?

  • Clean cardboard and scrap paper
  • Rinsed tin, steel, and aluminum cans
  • Rinsed #1 or #2 plastic bottles and jugs
  • Newspaper

What to consider:

  • Empty and dry your containers
  • Put cap or lid back on
  • Greasy pizza boxes cannot be recycled
  • Rules for food and beverage containers vary
  • Check before you chuck

What is not allowed to be recycled?

  • Bagged recyclables are not allowed. Empty them into your bin and reuse the bag.
  • Plastic wrap, bubble wrap, sandwich bags, and freezer bags cannot be recycled. Reuse these as much as possible.
  • Flexible mailer bags and packaging such as chip bags or juice pouches are made from multiple materials.
  • Polystyrene foam cannot be recycled. This includes packing peanuts, but shipping stores can take these back for reuse.
  • If you have non-recyclable items that cannot be reused, check with your local waste management.

Food waste? No problem!

  • While not all food waste can be composted, plenty of what gets left behind in recyclables or tossed in the garbage can actually give back to our environment.
  • Composting enriches soil, retains moisture, reduces overall waste, breaks down contamination, reduces chemical runoff, and helps prevent soil erosion.
  • Examples of compost include fruit and veggie scraps, egg shells, hair, coffee grounds, grass clippings, and dried leaves.
  • There are several different ways to create a compost pile, including constructing your own, or purchasing one at a home or garden store.

On a planetary scale...

We all need to work together to improve our recycling impact. Small steps can create a big movement if done properly. If you enjoy fresh air, clean yards, and a safe environment, you are not alone. Look into your local recycling initiative to ensure that you are within the guidelines in your town, and get started today.
